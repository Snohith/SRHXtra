For Harsh Dubey, the India call-up did not feel real straight away.

The 23-year-old left-arm spinner had spent years working through domestic cricket, trying to turn promising performances into something more substantial. When the news finally arrived that he had been picked for India's upcoming Afghanistan series, it took him a few minutes to fully process it.

Dubey's rise has been built largely on consistency. His standout Ranji Trophy campaign, in which he finished with 69 wickets, pushed him firmly into the national selection picture. His progress continued through Vidarbha, India A and the IPL, giving him experience across different levels of the game.

His association with Sunrisers Hyderabad also became an important part of that journey. After making three appearances for the franchise in 2025, Dubey earned a larger role the following season. That exposure, combined with his performances in domestic cricket, helped put him on the selectors' radar.

The spinner believes his time in Chennai's TNCA First Division was another important piece of the puzzle. Playing regularly on different surfaces and against strong opposition forced him to adjust his bowling, something he feels helped him develop as a more complete cricketer.

Those lessons became particularly valuable in first-class cricket. Dubey was also given the opportunity to lead Vidarbha and later spent time with India A. Each experience brought a different challenge, and together they helped him understand what might be expected at the international level.

There is understandable excitement surrounding his first chance with the senior side, but Dubey does not appear interested in getting carried away by the occasion. His focus is on being ready if captain and team management turn to him.

The all-rounder also sees his batting as a useful part of his game. Rather than being tied to one position, he is comfortable moving around the middle order according to the team's requirements. That flexibility could make him an attractive option in a squad looking for balance.

With the ball, however, his priorities remain straightforward. Dubey wants to attack the stumps, turn the ball and create wicket-taking opportunities — the approach that has served him throughout his domestic career.

His mindset is equally important to him. Results, he believes, are only one part of a cricketer's development. Preparation, discipline and the ability to keep improving are things he can control.

That attitude could prove useful as he moves into a new environment. International cricket will bring stronger opponents, greater scrutiny and unfamiliar pressures, but Dubey feels his journey through Vidarbha, domestic cricket, India A and the IPL has given him enough preparation to take the next step.

Now, after years of waiting for the opportunity, he has it. The next challenge is turning an India selection into an international career.
