India's first ODI against Afghanistan in Dharamsala was shortened by rain, but the reduced contest still gave two young players plenty to remember.

Gurnoor Brar and Harsh Dubey were handed their first ODI caps on Saturday, and both left the field with three wickets to their names as India opened the series with a seven-wicket win.

Brar made the stronger first impression.

The Punjab fast bowler, standing at 6ft 5in, was introduced early and quickly showed why his pace has attracted attention. He removed Ibrahim Zadran in his opening spell before returning at the end of the innings to account for Rashid Khan and Ziaur Rahman.

His final figures read 3 for 27 from 4.5 overs. More importantly, he consistently troubled Afghanistan's batters with speed and movement. The conditions were not easy after the long rain delay, yet Brar managed to make the most of his opportunity.

Afghanistan had been given only 25 overs to bat after the weather interrupted proceedings. Rahmanullah Gurbaz took full advantage of the shortened game, producing a rapid century and keeping his side in the contest almost single-handedly.

Gurbaz reached 102 from 48 deliveries, but once he departed, Afghanistan's innings lost momentum. The visitors eventually slipped to 194 all out in 24.5 overs.

Dubey's debut followed a different script.

The left-arm spinner had an expensive beginning, conceding 16 runs in his first over. Instead of changing his approach, though, he continued to attack the batters and eventually found his way into the wickets column.

He finished with 3 for 47 from five overs, helping India clean up the Afghanistan lower order. His figures also included an excellent catch from captain Shubman Gill, who reacted sharply to dismiss A M Ghazanfar.

For Dubey, the performance was another step in a rapid rise that began in domestic cricket. His outstanding Ranji Trophy campaign for Vidarbha, where he collected 69 wickets and contributed 476 runs during the title-winning season, had pushed him into the national conversation.

India's decision to hand both players their ODI debuts was part of a wider effort to look at emerging options. The BCCI had included Brar and Dubey in the ODI squad for the Afghanistan series, alongside several established names.

Brar's performance, in particular, gave India another reason to be encouraged. Captain Shubman Gill later praised the youngster's pace, control and ability to move the ball, while also backing Dubey for maintaining his attacking approach after an expensive start.

With 195 required for victory, India never allowed the chase to become uncomfortable. Gill took charge with an unbeaten 84, while KL Rahul supplied the finishing touches with a brisk 39 not out.

The result gave India a 1-0 lead in the three-match series.

For Brar and Dubey, however, the significance of the afternoon went beyond the result. Both had arrived in the Indian side as relatively new names at international level. By the end of their first ODI, both had already put their names on the scorecard in a substantial way.

The second match will be played in Lucknow on June 17, giving the pair another opportunity to show whether their impressive first outing was the beginning of something bigger.
