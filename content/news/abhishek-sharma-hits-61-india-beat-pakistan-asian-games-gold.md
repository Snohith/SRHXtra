Abhishek Sharma's 61 gives India a flying start against Pakistan

Abhishek Sharma walked into the Asian Games final against Pakistan and played the kind of innings India have come to expect from him in T20 cricket: quick, attacking and almost impossible to contain.

The left-hander made 61 from 28 balls at Korogi Athletic Park in Nisshin, striking three fours and seven sixes. His innings ended in the ninth over, but by then he had already given India the start they needed in the gold-medal match.

Abhishek reached his fifty in only 20 balls. Pakistan began with spin at both ends, but that did little to slow him down. Saad Masood's opening over disappeared for 24 runs, and Abhishek kept attacking as India looked to make the most of the powerplay.

His seven sixes were the standout feature of the innings. They took him level with Yuvraj Singh for the most sixes by a batter in a single India-Pakistan T20I innings. Yuvraj had also hit seven against Pakistan in Ahmedabad in 2012.

The latest performance also pushed Abhishek's total number of sixes against Pakistan in T20Is to 14. That moved him past Virat Kohli's previous tally of 11 and gave Abhishek the highest six count in matches between the two sides.

There was some fortune along the way. Reuters noted that Abhishek survived a run-out chance and saw two other attacking shots fall just short of Saim Ayub. Those moments did not change the way he approached the innings.

Arafat Minhas finally ended the assault when Abhishek was stumped. The wicket came after 28 balls, with the opener having already done enough to push India's scoring rate sharply upwards.

The innings also brought two milestones in Abhishek's wider T20 career. He completed 400 sixes in the format in 3,513 balls, making him the second-fastest batter to the mark behind Andre Russell. He also reached 100 T20 sixes in the 2026 calendar year in 651 balls, another mark bettered only by Russell.

Abhishek's 61 was not a long innings, but it was long enough to leave its mark on the final. He attacked from the start, took on Pakistan's spin-heavy plan and left India with a strong platform before his dismissal.

For an opener whose game is built around pressure from the first ball, it was another reminder of how quickly Abhishek Sharma can change the shape of a T20 innings.
