# Abhishek Sharma turns Delhi chase into a one-man assault

Abhishek Sharma did not give Afghanistan much time to settle in Delhi.

Chasing 157 in the first T20I, the left-hander launched an 82 off 32 balls, striking seven fours and seven sixes at a strike rate of 256.25. India reached the target with 38 balls still unused, but the chase had effectively been broken open by Abhishek's burst at the top.

He got his first boundary in the opening over, clearing the ropes against Fazalhaq Farooqi. Sanju Samson fell soon after, but the early wicket did nothing to change Abhishek's approach.

He kept looking for scoring options rather than trying to rebuild the innings.

The result was a half-century in 22 balls. Abhishek attacked both disciplines Afghanistan had hoped would slow him down: pace and spin. Rashid Khan, Noor Ahmad and Mohammad Nabi were all targeted, with the opener finding boundaries without allowing the spinners to build any real pressure. Against Afghanistan's spin contingent, he scored 46 from only 20 deliveries.

At the other end, Ishan Kishan matched enough of the tempo to keep the chase moving. The pair added 121 in just 53 balls for the second wicket, turning what could have been a conventional chase into a very short one.

Abhishek's innings was particularly notable for how little he seemed interested in playing within a set pattern. He attacked different bowlers in different areas, using the pace when it was available and taking on spin when Afghanistan tried to change the angle of attack.

By the time Mohammad Nabi finally dismissed him in the 11th over, Abhishek had already done almost all the damage. Nabi sent down a full toss, Abhishek went for the slog but failed to connect cleanly, and Rashid Khan completed the catch at wide long-on.

He walked off to a standing ovation after an innings that had taken India from a potentially routine chase to one that was almost over before Afghanistan could make it competitive.

For Abhishek, the 82 was also a useful response after a lean run. India had several options competing for places at the top of the order, but in Delhi he made the selection question considerably harder to ignore.
