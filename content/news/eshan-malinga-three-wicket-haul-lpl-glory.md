Eshan Malinga saved his best for the biggest night as Galle Gallants secured their first Lanka Premier League title with a five-wicket win over Jaffna Kings in Colombo.

The fast bowler returned figures of 3 for 25 from his four overs, playing a major part in restricting the defending champions to just 123 in the final at the R Premadasa Stadium.

Malinga struck at important moments and kept the Jaffna batting line-up from building any meaningful partnership. He removed Kamil Mishara, who had made 41, before sending back Shakib Al Hasan and Chamindu Wickramasinghe.

Jaffna had chosen to bat first, but their innings never really settled. Mishara provided the bulk of the resistance, while Wickramasinghe added 24 from 16 balls. Once wickets began falling regularly, Jaffna were bowled out in 19.3 overs.

Malinga was not the only bowler to make an impact. Charith Asalanka produced an equally important spell, taking 3 for 20. The pair accounted for six Jaffna wickets between them and ensured Galle were chasing a relatively modest target.

Galle's chase began badly.

Sam Harper was dismissed cheaply, and Asalanka followed soon after, leaving the Gallants at 10 for 2. For a side playing in its first LPL final after previous attempts had ended short of the trophy, the early wickets could easily have brought the pressure back onto them.

Thomas Fraser Rogers and Dinura Kalupahana changed the mood.

The pair put together 64 runs from 41 balls, giving Galle the breathing room they needed. Rogers made 34 from 27 deliveries, while Kalupahana attacked from the other end with 31 off 19.

Jaffna did find another opening when Shakib Al Hasan removed both Rogers and Kalupahana in quick succession. But the target was already within reach.

Dasun Shanaka then took responsibility for finishing the job. The Galle captain remained unbeaten on 20 from just nine balls and ended the contest with a boundary followed by a six.

Galle reached 126 for five in 15.5 overs, winning with 25 balls still unused.

The victory brought an end to Jaffna's run of dominance in the competition. The Kings had been chasing a fourth consecutive LPL championship, but Galle's disciplined bowling effort ensured that the title would change hands.

For Malinga, the final was a fitting finish to an impressive tournament. He ended the 2026 LPL season with 17 wickets from 10 matches, the highest tally in the competition according to the tournament statistics.

His three wickets in the final may not have earned him the Player of the Match award — that went to Asalanka for his 3 for 20 — but Malinga's spell was central to Galle gaining control of the contest.

The Gallants had waited for their first LPL crown. On the night that mattered most, their bowlers created the opening and their middle order finished the job.
