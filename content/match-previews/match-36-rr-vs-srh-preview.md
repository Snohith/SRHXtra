# Match context

Sunrisers Hyderabad travel to Jaipur to face Rajasthan Royals in Match 36 of IPL 2026. The fixture at the Sawai Mansingh Stadium on 25 April 2026 represents a meeting between two sides carrying significant momentum from their previous outings.

Rajasthan Royals come into this contest after a clinical 40-run victory over Lucknow Super Giants, while SRH arrive following a dominant 47-run win against Delhi Capitals. For both teams, this match is about phase control and adapting to one of the league's more multi-dimensional surfaces.

## Conditions and venue context

The Sawai Mansingh Stadium in Jaipur is historically regarded as a balanced surface that offers a nuanced contest between bat and ball. Unlike the more batter-friendly runways seen elsewhere in the tournament, Jaipur presents a different set of challenges. The large straight boundaries demand disciplined power-hitting, while the new ball often provides fast bowlers with enough early movement to disrupt top-order timing.

Recent statistical samples suggest a variance in scoring patterns. While historical averages hover around 167 for the first innings, the 2025 IPL sample at this venue was notably higher, with an average first-innings total of 198/5 across seven matches. All seven of those encounters saw totals of 200 or more, indicating that while the surface retains its balance, modern batting tempo can still force high-scoring outcomes. Spinners typically find grip and turn in the middle overs, making the 7-15 over phase a critical stretch for whichever side controls the rate.

## Recent form

**Rajasthan Royals** are in a phase of structural stability. Their 40-run win over LSG was built on a balanced collective effort rather than a single outlier performance. Yashasvi Jaiswal’s 22 and Ravindra Jadeja’s unbeaten 43 gave the innings weight, while the bowling unit defended 159 with precision. Jofra Archer’s 3/20 was the standout spell, supported by two wickets each from Nandre Burger and Brijesh Singh. RR's venue record in Jaipur (38 wins in 62 matches) underlines their comfort in these surroundings.

**Sunrisers Hyderabad** arrive with their batting engine firing at its highest ceiling. The 242 scored against Delhi Capitals was headlined by Abhishek Sharma’s 135 off 68 balls, a knock that redefined the tempo of the powerplay. Heinrich Klaasen’s quick-fire 37 off 13 underscored SRH’s ability to accelerate through the middle. With the ball, Eshan Malinga (4/32) and Harsh Dubey (3 wickets) proved they could defend a massive total even when the surface stayed true. SRH's Jaipur record is even, with 3 wins and 3 losses in 6 appearances.

## Key tactical battles

**Abhishek Sharma vs Jofra Archer:** The powerplay matchup between SRH’s most aggressive opener and RR’s premier pace threat will dictate the tone. Archer’s ability to extract bounce and movement could test Abhishek’s horizontal-bat shots early in the innings.

**Klaasen vs RR Spin:** The middle overs in Jaipur often reward spinners who can vary their pace. If RR can use the grip on the surface to slow Klaasen’s entry, it forces SRH into a more conservative rebuild—a phase RR usually controls with their venue familiarity.

**SRH's Seam Discipline vs RR’s Stability:** Eshan Malinga’s wicket-taking form meets a Rajasthan top order that prioritizes stability. If SRH can strike early at the Sawai Mansingh Stadium, they can bring RR's middle order under pressure before the field spreads.

## Probable decisions / team questions

No confirmed XIs were available at the time of writing. The central question for SRH remains the balance between their aggressive batting ceiling and the bowling coverage required for Jaipur's larger dimensions. Rajasthan’s structural stability suggests they are unlikely to make radical changes, relying instead on their established roles and venue comfort.

Impact Player strategies will likely be dictated by the toss. Given the historical edge for teams batting second in Jaipur (4 wins to 3 in the 2025 sample), both sides will need a clear plan for managing dew if it arrives in the second innings.

## X-factor

**Abhishek Sharma (SRH):** His capacity to remove scoreboard pressure inside the first six overs makes him the primary tactical hinge. If he settles, Jaipur’s large boundaries become less of a defensive asset for RR.

**Jofra Archer (RR):** In a venue where pace can find early purchase, Archer is the bowler most capable of disrupting SRH's preferred powerplay launch.

## Verdict

This is a contest between SRH’s high batting ceiling and Rajasthan’s structural stability and venue comfort. While SRH have the firepower to blow the game open early, Jaipur rewards the side that manages the middle-over spin phase more effectively. SRH's success depends on whether their bowlers can replicate their recent clinical finishing against a Rajasthan side that rarely beats itself.

**SRHXtra read:** A phase-control battle where the first six overs and the 7-15 over spin stretch will likely decide the outcome. SRH's aggression meets its most disciplined test yet.
