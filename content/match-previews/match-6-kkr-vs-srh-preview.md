# Match context

SRH's second fixture of IPL 2026 takes them to Eden Gardens for Match 6 against Kolkata Knight Riders. The official schedule lists a 19:30 IST start on 2 April 2026, with KKR at home.

The early SRH question is not whether the batting has enough power. It clearly does. The sharper question is whether the innings can start with control before turning into acceleration. Against KKR, that matters because Ajinkya Rahane's side have enough spin and pace variety to punish a top order that chases tempo before reading conditions.

## Conditions and venue context

The official KKR page lists Eden Gardens as Kolkata's venue. No official pitch note was available in the checked sources, so this preview avoids a surface-specific prediction.

The venue still changes the tactical demand. Eden Gardens usually rewards batters who use pace well, but the square dimensions and spin options can make middle-over hitting less automatic than it looks from the outside. SRH need to avoid treating every over as a launch point.

## Recent form

For SRH, this is about response. The first outing left enough evidence that the middle order can rebuild, but also enough warning that early wickets can pull their preferred shape apart.

KKR's squad profile is built around flexible batting and spin pressure. Rahane, Rinku Singh, Sunil Narine, Varun Chakaravarthy, and the seam group give them multiple ways to slow an innings without needing a long spell of dominance.

## Key tactical battles

**SRH openers vs KKR's matchup bowling:** Abhishek Sharma and Travis Head are still SRH's cleanest path to a commanding innings. KKR will want to deny width and bring spin into the game before SRH's left-hand pair settle into hitting arcs.

**Klaasen against Varun and Narine:** This is the middle-over hinge. If Klaasen gets a stable entry point, SRH can attack spin without losing structure. If he has to rebuild from a collapse, KKR can use their spinners to make the innings narrower.

**SRH seamers vs KKR's finishers:** KKR have enough lower-order power to make the last five overs uncomfortable. SRH's death bowling needs slower-ball discipline and field-side clarity rather than simply pace into the pitch.

## Probable decisions / team questions

No confirmed XI was available at writing time. SRH's key decision is whether to add an extra stabilizer or trust their core batting model and strengthen the bowling.

The Impact Player call should depend on toss and innings order. Away from home, SRH may value a bowling option who can cover one bad matchup instead of loading only for batting depth.

## X-factor

Heinrich Klaasen is SRH's X-factor because Eden Gardens can become a spin-control game in the middle. If Klaasen disrupts that phase, SRH's batting order starts to look much deeper than the scoreboard suggests.

For KKR, Varun Chakaravarthy is the pressure point. If he forces SRH to take risks from one end, KKR can manage the rest of the innings with more aggressive fields.

## Verdict

This is an early but useful test of SRH's adaptability. The batting ceiling travels, but only if the first six overs are clean enough to let the middle order attack by choice rather than necessity.

**SRHXtra read:** SRH can control this if the top order respects the first spell and Klaasen gets a platform against spin.
