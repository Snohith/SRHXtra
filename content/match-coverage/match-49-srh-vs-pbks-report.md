# Match context

SRH needed a clean response after the KKR defeat, and they found it through the exact shape that has defined their best cricket this season: top-order acceleration, middle-over pressure, and a defence led by Pat Cummins. At the Rajiv Gandhi International Stadium, Hyderabad, SRH made 235/4 before holding Punjab Kings to 202/7 for a 33-run win.

The result mattered beyond the two points. It moved SRH to the top of the IPL 2026 points table and restored the feeling that their batting depth and bowling control can work together rather than as separate strengths.

## Powerplay launch

Abhishek Sharma and Travis Head gave SRH another violent first act. Abhishek's 35 off 13 and Head's 38 off 19 meant Punjab were pulled into defensive choices almost immediately, with the opening stand racing to 54 in 3.2 overs.

Even after Abhishek fell, Head kept the scoring rate high enough for SRH to reach 79/1 at the end of the powerplay. That phase did two things at once: it gave the innings a 230-plus ceiling and forced PBKS to search for containment before the middle overs had properly begun.

## Ishan and Klaasen control the middle

Once Yuzvendra Chahal removed Head, the innings could have drifted into a reset. Ishan Kishan and Heinrich Klaasen did not allow that. Their 88-run third-wicket partnership carried SRH through the middle while still keeping boundary pressure on Punjab's attack.

Ishan's 55 gave the innings tempo from one end, including a sharp acceleration against Vijaykumar Vyshak. Klaasen, unbeaten on 69 off 43, supplied the more sustained threat. The partnership was not just about scoring quickly; it made PBKS keep changing plans without ever fully dragging SRH below par.

## Finish beyond 230

After Ishan fell to Arshdeep Singh, SRH still had finishing work to do. Klaasen found the right partner in Nitish Kumar Reddy, whose unbeaten 29 off 13 gave the final five overs the lift they needed.

The Klaasen-NKR stand added 63 late runs and pushed SRH to 235/4. On a true Hyderabad surface, that was not an impossible target, but it was a total that demanded a near-perfect chase.

## Cummins breaks the chase early

PBKS never got that clean chase because SRH's new-ball pressure changed the innings. Eshan Malinga and Cummins were involved in sharp early catches, and Cummins then struck twice with the ball. By the fourth over, PBKS were 23/3.

That start was decisive because it made the required rate and the wicket column pull in opposite directions. Cummins finished with 2/34 and gave the defence a calm centre. Shivang Kumar's 2/45 added another layer of control as Punjab tried to rebuild.

## Connolly keeps Punjab alive

Cooper Connolly made SRH work for the win. His unbeaten 107 was a maiden T20/IPL century and a serious innings under scoreboard pressure. He found some support from Marcus Stoinis and Suryansh Shedge, but PBKS never fully escaped the damage of that 23/3 start.

That is why this result should not be read as a one-sided cruise. SRH had the stronger all-round night, but Connolly's resistance kept the game honest deep enough for the defence to stay switched on.

## Why the win matters

This was a useful SRH win because it was not built on one phase alone. Abhishek and Head opened the ceiling, Ishan and Klaasen owned the middle, Klaasen and NKR finished, and Cummins gave the bowlers the early wickets they needed.

The larger table implication is clear: SRH moved to the top, and they did it by showing depth in both departments. The next test is away from home. SRH travel to Ahmedabad to face Gujarat Titans on 12 May at the Narendra Modi Stadium, where protecting momentum and playoff positioning becomes the next layer of the campaign.
