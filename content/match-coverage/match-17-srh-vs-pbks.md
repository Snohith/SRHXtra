# Match context

This was a high-scoring game in which both sides won the same phase once and only one side sustained it. SRH's first six overs were exceptional. Punjab Kings' response was to drag the innings back into a manageable range and then reproduce the same aggression with greater continuity in the chase.

Punjab won the toss on 11 April 2026, chose to field in New Chandigarh, and then chased 220 with seven balls unused.

## Toss and team direction

Putting SRH in did not look like the right choice during the powerplay. Abhishek Sharma and Travis Head attacked immediately and took SRH to 105/0 in six overs, which matched the third-highest powerplay score in IPL history.

But the chase-sized target everyone expected at that stage never fully materialized because Punjab found wickets and control straight after the field spread.

## First innings summary

Abhishek's 74 from 28 and Head's 38 from 23 gave SRH total authority over the first eight overs. The opening stand of 120 made 230-plus look realistic and left Punjab reacting rather than shaping the game.

The key change came in the ninth over, when Shashank Singh removed both openers. That double strike did not just end the partnership; it changed the tempo of the innings. SRH scored 77/3 in the next ten overs and never recovered the same fluency.

Ishan Kishan and Heinrich Klaasen added 47 from 33 in an attempt to restabilize, but Kishan's dismissal after Marco Jansen's catch at deep midwicket was another important stop. Klaasen made 39 from 33, Aniket Verma added 18 from 9, and SRH closed on 219/6. That was still a strong score, but it was short of what 105/0 after six suggested was available.

## Second innings summary

Punjab's answer was to chase with the same intent SRH had shown. Priyansh Arya and Prabhsimran Singh drove PBKS to 93/0 in the powerplay, immediately cancelling the scoreboard pressure that should have come with a 220 chase.

Arya made 57 from 20 and Prabhsimran 51 from 25 before both fell. The match then moved to its middle-overs question: whether Punjab would lose momentum after the opening pair. They did not, because Shreyas Iyer controlled that phase far better than SRH had in the first innings.

Iyer's unbeaten 69 from 33, including a 69-run partnership with Nehal Wadhera, took the chase out of its most dangerous zone. Once the equation dropped to 23 off 23, SRH no longer had enough leverage to force a collapse, and Punjab finished at 223/4 in 18.5 overs.

## Tactical turning points

- SRH's 105/0 powerplay established a huge scoring platform.
- Shashank Singh's double strike in the ninth over changed the innings from expansion to recovery.
- Punjab's own 93/0 powerplay removed most of the scoreboard pressure from the chase.
- Shreyas Iyer's middle-overs control separated Punjab's chase from SRH's first-innings slowdown.

## Standout performers

- Abhishek Sharma's 74 from 28 was the main force behind SRH's powerplay dominance.
- Shashank Singh's wickets of both openers were the most important breakthroughs of the first innings.
- Priyansh Arya and Prabhsimran Singh gave Punjab a matching start in the chase.
- Shreyas Iyer's unbeaten 69 from 33 made him the decisive batter once the game moved past the powerplay.

## Final result

Punjab Kings beat Sunrisers Hyderabad by 6 wickets. SRH scored 219/6 and PBKS replied with 223/4 in 18.5 overs.

## Neutral conclusion

The match showed the difference between a fast start and a complete innings. SRH won the first six overs emphatically, but Punjab responded in the same style and then handled the transition to the middle overs with far more control.
