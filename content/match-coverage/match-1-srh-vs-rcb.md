# Match context

SRH opened IPL 2026 with a score that was competitive on paper and fragile in context. Royal Challengers Bengaluru won the toss on 28 March 2026 and chose to field at the M Chinnaswamy Stadium, then controlled the game's highest-leverage passages to win by six wickets.

The final scoreboard read SRH 201/9 and RCB 203/4 in 15.4 overs. That margin mattered because SRH's recovery with the bat was strong enough to create a contest, but the chase showed how quickly control can disappear on a surface where the new ball slides on and the outfield rewards clean contact.

## Toss and team direction

RCB's decision to bowl first was validated almost immediately. Jacob Duffy's opening spell attacked SRH with hard lengths and enough movement to stop the visitors from getting their preferred powerplay launch.

SRH were 21 for 3 inside the first six overs after the wickets of Abhishek Sharma, Travis Head, and Nitish Kumar Reddy. That start forced a reset rather than the usual top-order surge.

## First innings summary

The recovery came through Ishan Kishan and Heinrich Klaasen, and it was substantial enough to change the mood of the innings. Their 97-run partnership from 53 balls restored tempo and pushed SRH back toward a par-plus score instead of a below-par salvage job.

Klaasen's 31 from 22 gave the stand its first burst. Kishan then carried the larger scoring load, making 80 from 38 and keeping the innings moving even after Klaasen fell in the 14th over. SRH were able to attack pace and spin once that pair stabilized the middle phase.

Aniket Verma's 43 from 18 added the late acceleration that took SRH beyond 200. Even so, the innings always carried the trace of the damage done in the powerplay. Duffy finished with 3/22 and Romario Shepherd added 3/54, while two boundary catches from Phil Salt cut short SRH's two most important innings at moments that prevented an even larger total.

## Second innings summary

RCB's chase was shaped by how quickly they removed scoreboard pressure. Phil Salt fell early, but Devdutt Padikkal and Virat Kohli turned the pursuit into a run-rate advantage almost immediately, adding 101 in just 45 balls.

Padikkal's 61 from 26 was the innings' main surge. Kohli's role was different but equally important: he absorbed very little pressure, kept the chase stable, and remained unbeaten on 69 from 38. Once Rajat Patidar made 31 from 12 in a 53-run stand with Kohli, the equation had moved decisively away from SRH.

RCB reached 203/4 with 26 balls unused, which underlined how little time SRH's attack had to apply pressure after the field spread.

## Tactical turning points

- Duffy's three early wickets changed SRH from an attacking powerplay side into a rebuilding side.
- The Kishan-Klaasen stand restored the innings, but Salt's boundary catches ended both set batters before SRH could cash in for a 215-plus finish.
- RCB's 101-run second-wicket stand meant the chase never entered a phase where SRH could force risk through required-rate pressure.

## Standout performers

- Ishan Kishan gave SRH their route back into the game with 80 off 38 after the early collapse.
- Heinrich Klaasen's 31 off 22 was a high-value counterattack because it arrived while SRH were still recovering from 21 for 3.
- Jacob Duffy's 3/22 was the most important bowling spell of the match.
- Devdutt Padikkal and Virat Kohli controlled the chase, with Kohli finishing unbeaten on 69.

## Final result

Royal Challengers Bengaluru beat Sunrisers Hyderabad by 6 wickets. SRH scored 201/9 and RCB replied with 203/4 in 15.4 overs.

## Neutral conclusion

SRH showed resilience with the bat after the early collapse, but the match was decided by phase control. RCB won the new-ball exchange, then turned the chase into a one-way scoring pattern before SRH could recover enough bowling leverage.
