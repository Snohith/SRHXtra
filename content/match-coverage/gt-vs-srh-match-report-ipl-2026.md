# Match summary

SRH's Ahmedabad trip turned into a heavy reset. Gujarat Titans beat Sunrisers Hyderabad by 82 runs at the Narendra Modi Stadium on 12 May 2026, turning a strong scoreboard position into sustained pressure and then into a chase that never found stable ground.

For Hyderabad, the result hurts because it arrived at a point where the campaign had started to feel controlled. The previous win over Punjab Kings had shown SRH's most complete version: early batting tempo, middle-order control, and new-ball authority. Ahmedabad offered a different kind of test, and GT forced SRH into the uncomfortable side of it.

This was not just a defeat by margin. It was a defeat by rhythm. Gujarat made SRH play from behind, then kept removing the small windows Hyderabad needed to rebuild the chase.

## Where the chase broke

The chase broke when SRH lost control of tempo. Against a target shaped by scoreboard pressure, Hyderabad needed one clean top-order platform: either a fast powerplay that changed the field, or a calmer start that kept wickets intact for the middle overs.

They found neither for long enough. Once the chase started to tilt, SRH's batting began to look like it was solving the required rate and the wicket column at the same time. That is a difficult balance anywhere, but Ahmedabad made it harsher because every quiet over increased the demand on the next batter.

The larger issue was that GT never allowed Hyderabad to settle into their preferred pattern. SRH are at their best when Abhishek Sharma and Travis Head create early panic, then Ishan Kishan and Heinrich Klaasen inherit a game already moving at speed. In this match, the middle order had to absorb pressure before it could apply any.

## Tactical issue for SRH

The tactical concern is not that SRH lost while chasing. It is that the chase became too dependent on instant correction. When the first burst does not land, Hyderabad need a second method that keeps them alive without turning every over into a boundary hunt.

That means strike rotation, better use of the bigger square pockets, and clearer risk selection against bowlers who are trying to drag the innings away from pace-on hitting. SRH's power game is still their strength, but it becomes easier to defend when the opposition can predict when the release shot is coming.

The bowling unit will also look back at how GT built enough scoreboard control to make the chase so steep. Hyderabad do not need to overreact, but they do need tighter phase management away from home: fewer release balls after a good over, cleaner fielding support, and more pressure carried from one bowler to the next.

## What the loss changes

The 82-run margin changes the feeling around the next fixture. It does not erase the good work SRH had done before Ahmedabad, but it does make the Chennai trip more important as a response game. A side chasing playoff position cannot let one heavy defeat become two uncertain performances.

It also sharpens the net run rate conversation. Big margins matter late in the league phase, and SRH know that an 82-run loss is not only a result in the loss column. It is a reminder that control has to travel, especially when the schedule asks for back-to-back away challenges.

The selection conversation should stay measured. One bad chase does not mean the batting identity is broken. The more useful question is whether the same structure can give itself better fallback options when the first six overs do not go Hyderabad's way.

## Next response

The response now moves to Chennai. CSK at MA Chidambaram Stadium is exactly the sort of fixture that can test whether SRH have absorbed the lesson from Ahmedabad. Chepauk will not always reward one-speed hitting, and it will ask Hyderabad to stay calm when the game slows.

SRH's next response has to be built around phase discipline. With the bat, that means keeping intent but protecting the innings from a second collapse. With the ball, it means making early overs count and not letting CSK ease into middle-over control.

Ahmedabad was a hard night, but it can still be useful if SRH treat it as information rather than noise. The core of the side remains dangerous. The next step is making sure that danger comes with enough control to survive pressure, not only enough power to dominate when the game is already flowing their way.
