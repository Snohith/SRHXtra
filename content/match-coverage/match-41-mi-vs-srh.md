# Match context

Mumbai Indians chose to bat and posted 243/5 at the Wankhede Stadium. SRH chased it down at 249/4 in 18.4 overs, winning by six wickets and extending their run of victories.

The official report frames the match as a batting-heavy contest. Ryan Rickelton's unbeaten 123 gave Mumbai the first major statement, but SRH's top order and middle order kept the chase ahead of the rate for long enough to finish with eight balls left.

## Toss and team direction

Mumbai's decision to bat was backed up by a 78-run powerplay and a dominant Rickelton innings. Will Jacks made 46, Hardik Pandya added 31 off 15, and Mumbai reached a total that usually demands near-perfect chasing.

SRH's reply was not built around waiting for a late surge. Travis Head and Abhishek Sharma attacked the new ball, forcing Mumbai to defend 244 from behind almost immediately.

## First innings summary

Rickelton finished unbeaten on 123 off 55 balls, with 10 fours and 8 sixes. His innings carried Mumbai through the middle and death overs after the opening phase had already set a high scoring base.

Hardik's 31 off 15 helped Mumbai cross 240. For SRH, Eshan Malinga was the most controlled bowler in the official figures at 1/29 from four overs, while Praful Hinge took two wickets in a difficult innings for bowlers.

## Second innings summary

SRH's openers made the chase credible straight away. Head and Abhishek took the score to 92/0 in the powerplay and 126/0 by the eighth over, keeping the required rate from becoming the central pressure point.

Mumbai briefly reopened the contest when Allah Ghazanfar removed Abhishek and Ishan Kishan on consecutive balls before Hardik dismissed Head for 76 off 30. Klaasen then restored SRH's control with 56 off 23, Nitish Kumar Reddy made 21, and Salil Arora finished unbeaten on 30 off 10.

## Tactical turning points

- Rickelton's unbeaten 123 turned Mumbai's strong start into a 243/5 total.
- SRH's 92-run powerplay meant the chase never needed a late rescue from an impossible rate.
- Mumbai's three wickets for four runs created the main defensive opening of the match.
- Klaasen and Nitish rebuilt through an 80-run stand before Salil closed the chase.

## Standout performers

- Official player of the match: Heinrich Klaasen.
- Heinrich Klaasen: 56 off 23 balls.
- Travis Head: 76 off 30 balls.
- Salil Arora: 30 not out off 10 balls.
- Ryan Rickelton: 123 not out off 55 balls for Mumbai Indians.

## Neutral closing takeaway

The difference was not that Mumbai underhit their total; 243/5 was a serious score. SRH won because their chase had three layers: an opening assault, a Klaasen-led reset after wickets, and a short finishing burst that kept the result away from the final over.
