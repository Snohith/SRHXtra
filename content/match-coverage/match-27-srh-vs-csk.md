# Match context

This was the kind of defense that depended less on one overwhelming spell and more on repeated interruptions to the chase. SRH made 194/9 in Hyderabad on 18 April 2026, then prevented Chennai Super Kings from finding a long run of control even when the target stayed within range at halfway.

CSK won the toss and chose to field. SRH still reached a defendable score because they produced two different productive phases: a powerplay burst through Abhishek Sharma and a middle-overs rebuild through Heinrich Klaasen.

## Toss and team direction

Chennai's choice to chase was understandable on a ground where targets can move quickly. SRH's response was to attack the first six overs hard again, reaching 75/2 despite losing two wickets in Mukesh Choudhary's double-strike over near the end of the powerplay.

That left the innings at an interesting point: fast enough to threaten 200, but vulnerable to a further middle-overs stall.

## First innings summary

Abhishek Sharma's 59 from 22 carried the launch phase. Travis Head added 23 from 20 in the opening stand of 75 from 34 balls, but Mukesh's wickets of Head and Ishan Kishan in successive deliveries abruptly changed the innings' direction.

Jamie Overton then removed Abhishek to leave SRH 93/3 in eight overs. That could easily have become the innings' collapse point, but Klaasen rebuilt calmly and decisively. His 59 from 39 was the innings that gave shape to the rest of the scorecard.

Klaasen built smaller but useful stands with Aniket Verma, Nitish Kumar Reddy, and Salil Arora, making sure SRH kept moving rather than falling into a long middle-overs slowdown. CSK still finished the innings well through Overton's 3/37 and Anshul Kamboj's 3/22, with SRH managing only 31/4 in the final four overs, but 194 remained enough to defend if wickets arrived regularly.

## Second innings summary

CSK's chase had a burst of momentum in the first four overs. Nitish Kumar Reddy removed Sanju Samson early, but Ayush Mhatre's 30 from 13 pushed Chennai to 60/1 and briefly tilted the match toward the chase.

That phase ended when Mhatre's injury-affected dismissal gave Reddy a second wicket, and Eshan Malinga then removed Ruturaj Gaikwad first ball. Sarfaraz Khan and Matt Short rebuilt with 46 runs, and at the ten-over mark CSK still needed only 84 from 60 balls with seven wickets in hand.

The difference from there was SRH's ability to keep puncturing the innings before one settled pair could finish the game. Sarfaraz fell in the 11th over, Dewald Brevis made a duck, Short never fully accelerated, and Shivam Dube's 21 from 16 was not enough to drag the required rate back under control. CSK closed on 184/8, 10 short of the target.

## Tactical turning points

- Mukesh Choudhary's double strike kept SRH from fully cashing in on another rapid start.
- Klaasen's 59 prevented SRH from sliding below a defendable total after 93/3.
- Nitish Kumar Reddy's wickets of Samson and Mhatre stopped CSK's first burst from becoming a dominant start.
- SRH kept taking wickets around the halfway mark of the chase, which denied CSK the one uninterrupted stand the target required.

## Standout performers

- Abhishek Sharma's 59 from 22 set SRH's tempo early.
- Heinrich Klaasen's 59 from 39 was the innings' stabilizing effort.
- Nitish Kumar Reddy's early wickets changed the shape of the chase.
- Eshan Malinga, the official player of the match, supplied the middle-overs breakthroughs that held the defense together.

## Final result

Sunrisers Hyderabad beat Chennai Super Kings by 10 runs. SRH scored 194/9 and held CSK to 184/8.

## Neutral conclusion

SRH did not dominate every phase, but they were better at recovering the game each time it threatened to swing away. Klaasen's rebuild gave them a defendable score, and the bowlers then made sure Chennai were always chasing the target in fragments rather than on their own terms.
